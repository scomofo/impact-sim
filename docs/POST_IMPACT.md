# Post-impact effects: evidence and visual limits

Scientific visual audit, 6 September 2026. Read alongside [MODEL.md](MODEL.md).

The earlier animation implied consequences that the numerical model does not calculate. This revision removes those implications and clarifies what remains schematic. The analytical assessment model stays at **0.2.0**. No general atmospheric transport, climate, ejecta-reentry radiation, wildfire or coupled Earth-system simulator has been added. A removed effect means **not calculated**, not physically impossible or absent.

## What the animation can support

| Earlier visual claim | Modelled quantity or missing calculation | Correction and remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A gray veil appears everywhere and immediately dims sunlight. | Dust opacity came from an energy severity band. There is no particle inventory, injection altitude, atmospheric transport, optical depth or radiative forcing calculation. | Remove the global dust veil and associated sunlight dimming. Do not replace them with an invented universal dispersal speed. |
| An ignition front sweeps through the thermal footprint. | `burnRadius()` estimates the distance to a human burn-exposure threshold under an idealized visible-fireball model; it is not a wildfire perimeter or a prediction of molten ground. | Show a static exposure highlight at the calculated angular radius, without a minimum radius that enlarges a small event. Its appearance and fade are presentation choices, not fire propagation or cooling rates. |
| Wave-ring shading can make a whole region appear filled at once. | The ring is an illustration; its geometry is not a computed pressure, temperature or displacement field. | Correct the shader's ring mask. Ring travel and width remain schematic, and must not define a hazard boundary. |
| A large impact guarantees a fiery explosion on the opposite side of Earth. | The assessment provides an approximate seismic intensity at distance. It does not solve antipodal focusing, crustal rupture or ejection. | Remove the scheduled antipodal flash and hot ejecta. Focusing is a legitimate research topic, but this app cannot predict its consequences. |
| Random secondary explosions and craters are predicted fallback strikes. | The particle display and random strike schedule are not a mass-conserving ballistic deposition or secondary-cratering calculation. | Remove the random secondary-strike events. Remaining ejecta particles illustrate material leaving the impact site; their count, glow and visible lifetime are not measurements or reentry-heating predictions. |
| Every giant-impact case produces global surface incandescence or removes the atmosphere. | Giant-impact outcomes use simplified collision scaling and heuristic melt/disk quantities, without a spatial temperature field or atmospheric-loss solver. | Remove unconditional global heating and atmosphere-loss choreography. Giant impacts remain outside the regional assessment's validation, and absence of a rendered consequence is not a retention or habitability prediction. |
| One short timeline predicts the sequence and duration of all aftermath effects. | The renderer combines independently compressed animation timings; there is no common physical post-impact clock. | Label it as animation time and carry visual limitations into the interface and report. Calculated observer arrival times, where available, are separate numerical estimates. |

Tsunami rings, debris disks, moon accretion, crater appearance and particle motion remain **schematic**. The tsunami calculation has no coastline, bathymetry or inundation model. The giant-impact disk and moon illustration does not establish that a stable satellite will form, its formation time, or its final orbit. Existing analytical and heuristic limitations in [MODEL.md](MODEL.md) still apply.

## What the literature supports

**Primary fireball exposure and subsequent fires are different calculations.** The implemented regional thermal equations follow [Collins, Melosh & Marcus (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1945-5100.2005.tb00157.x), with an idealized fireball and horizon correction. They do not include delayed radiation from reentering ejecta. Laboratory experiments using impact-like heat pulses found that ignition depends on fuel condition and pulse duration, with substantial differences between locations and fuels. A burn-exposure contour therefore cannot be presented as an advancing continuous fire front. [Belcher et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1144/jgs2014-082)

**Rapid ejecta dispersal is not immediate uniform coverage.** Chicxulub simulations by Artemieva & Morgan describe a fast dust cloud produced through ejecta-atmosphere interactions, with some material reaching about 8,000 km after an hour. Their calculation does not resolve an entire globe's subsequent transport and does not establish one spreading law for arbitrary impacts. Ballistic ejecta, a moving atmospheric cloud and longer-lived aerosols must be distinguished. [Artemieva & Morgan (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019GL086562)

**Antipodal focusing does not establish an antipodal explosion.** A Chicxulub-scale seismic study found that realistic Earth structure and a distributed source reduce focusing relative to an idealized spherical point-source model. It reports displacement, stress and strain; those results cannot justify the app's former flash and incandescent particles at the antipode. [Meschede, Myhrvold & Tromp (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-246X.2011.05170.x)

Recent Chicxulub studies also show why climate and reentry heating need separate, explicitly scoped scenarios:

| Study | Scoped finding | Implication for this app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Senel et al. (2023), fine silicate dust](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-023-01290-4) | Palaeoclimate simulations constrained by a K-Pg deposit's particle-size distribution found a substantial contribution from fine dust. Their scenarios include long dust residence and prolonged suppression of photosynthetically active light. | These are material- and scenario-dependent climate results, not an energy-to-darkness conversion or a prediction for every impact. |
| [Rodiouchkina et al. (2025), sulfur release](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-55145-6) | Sulfur concentrations and isotopic evidence give an estimated Chicxulub release of 67 ± 39 Gt, below a prominent earlier numerical estimate. The authors discuss a potentially smaller sulfur contribution to impact winter. | Target composition and uncertain material budgets matter. Lower sulfur does not establish that dust, soot or all climate effects were negligible. |
| [Johnson et al. (28 July 2026), heating enhanced by fine dust](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026JG009837) | Their analysis argues that fine dust accompanying reentering spherules can enhance radiation reaching the surface, strengthening the case for widespread heat and fires. | This is a current research interpretation, not a generic validated heating function. It does not license an immediate global glow for a chosen severity band. |

Reentry heating and the extent of fires remain sensitive to ejecta delivery and radiative-transfer assumptions. [Morgan, Artemieva & Goldin (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013JG002428) found strong geographic differences and argued against globally ignited wildfires under their simulations; the 2026 work above offers a different interpretation incorporating fine dust. The app must not describe either universal global ignition or the absence of global reentry heating as settled. It currently calculates neither.

## Next validation gates

1. **Renderer check on real hardware:** inspect small crater, airburst, ocean, Chicxulub-scale and giant scenarios in Chrome and Safari with working WebGL. Check opposite-side views, pause, replay and scrubbing. Verify there is no global veil, unmodelled flash, persistent global emissive material or ring-mask artifact. Automated policy tests do not establish GPU rendering correctness.
2. **Assessment-to-visual agreement:** compare the static exposure radius with `burnRadius()` at several distances and scales, including unavailable/zero exposure and an event smaller than a display pixel. Validate that the overlay does not imply additional physical reach to remain visible. Confirm animation timing cannot be confused with numerical arrival time in the UI or export.
3. **Independent numerical review:** retain published-equation comparisons, but also assess applicability ranges and discrepancies against independent observations or simulations. Matching an analytical reference is not validation of atmospheric aftermath, event reconstruction or giant-impact outcomes.
4. **Evidence gate before adding atmospheric consequences:** require documented material budgets, particle sizes, injection profiles, transport and radiative assumptions, an explicit physical timescale, and suitable reference outputs. Record missing mechanisms and uncertainty. Do not make unsupported values appear as zero.

A possible next feature is a **separate layer of published scenarios**, starting with reproducible Chicxulub studies. It would identify each study, dataset, target assumptions and physical time axis; show competing estimates where relevant; and keep those results distinct from the editable generic impact assessment. Eligibility would depend on obtaining and checking suitable data and reuse terms. Interpolation would require demonstrated coverage of the chosen parameters; arbitrary extrapolation from one historical event would remain unavailable. This proposal is not an implemented model or a claim that a general aftermath solver can be inferred from the existing inputs.
